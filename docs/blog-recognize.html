When should a model trust its own match?

Re:Cognize evaluates comic character re-identification while the story is read. Recognising a character already seen proves to be within reach. Deciding which of its own matches a model should remember does not, and that decision, rather than the visual representation, sets the limit.

The maintainers · 18 November 2026 · 8 min read

Paper

Aaditya Baranwal, Madhav Kataria, Yogesh S. Rawat and Shruti Vyas. Re:Cognize – A Framework For Open-Set Comic Character Re-Identification. NeurIPS 2026, Evaluations and Datasets Track.

arXiv ↗Code ↗Project page ↗

A reader of a manga holds its cast in mind for hundreds of pages. A character introduced in the first chapter is recognised in the tenth, redrawn from another angle and in other clothes, even when the story never names them, and without any list of who appears in the volume. Searching comics, indexing them, answering questions about their stories and describing them for blind and low-vision readers all rest on this capability.

Character re-identification (Re-ID) is the task meant to provide it, yet its standard evaluation assumes something a reader never has. Each query is matched against a gallery of reference crops, built for a fixed set of identities before the first query arrives. A volume read for the first time offers no such gallery. New characters appear before they are named, and their designs drift across story arcs, so a model has to infer the cast while it reads. Re:Cognize turns that requirement into an evaluation, and our NeurIPS 2026 paper reports what it reveals.

One stream, four galleries

Re:Cognize presents a volume as a single stream of character crops in reading order. For each crop, the query, a system must either identify the character from its gallery or recognise that the character is new. Two coupled sub-problems follow: identity emergence, for a character not yet observed, and identity maintenance, for one already encountered. Maintenance accounts for most of the stream. Four protocols answer the same stream and differ only in the gallery and in whether it may change.

ProtocolGallery at the startDuring the stream
P1 closed setA fifth of every character's cropsFixed. The closed-set ceiling, scored by mAP and Rank-k
P2 seededk labelled crops per character, k = 1 to 5, drawn at random or taken from first appearancesFixed
P3 emptyNothingEach crop joins the nearest cluster above a novelty threshold or opens a new one. A diagnostic of emergence
P4 seeded, growingThe seeds of P2Each query is added under the label of its top-1 match, up to 50 additions per character

Comparing P1 with P2 asks how much of a closed-set gallery a few seeds can replace. Comparing three policies within P4, at the same number of seeds, asks what the stream itself can contribute: the static policy keeps the seeded gallery, the predicted policy adds each query under its top-1 label, and the oracle adds it under its true label. A weak representation would depress both comparisons, so when they diverge, the deficit lies in the gallery rather than in the encoder. P4 is scored by identity Rank-1, the share of queries whose top-ranked character is correct.

We apply the protocols to three corpora of Japanese comics, POPCharacters, Manga109 and Re:Verse, with nearly 44,000 crops from 51 series and volumes. We score five Re-ID backbones that span three regimes: TransReID and InstructReID from person Re-ID, the manga-native MagiV2 and MagiV3, and the CLIP-based ReID5o. Each runs alone and with MeCha, a maintenance baseline we introduce that equips any frozen backbone with a working and an episodic memory of each character.

Recognition is within reach, growth is not

A small gallery suffices for ranking. On the eight held-out POPCharacters series, a single seed per character reaches 102 to 107 percent of the closed-set mAP on all five backbones with MeCha, and 43 to 66 percent of its Rank-1.

Growth exposes the deficit. From one random seed per character, growing the gallery with the model's own top-1 matches lowers identity Rank-1 for every backbone and configuration we test, whereas growing it with the true labels would raise identity Rank-1 by 22 to 27 points. The cause is contamination: the model assigns 62 to 89 percent of its additions to the wrong character. The loss persists through every quarter of the stream, which makes it a failure of acceptance rather than of gradual drift. Confidence does not separate the two cases, since correct and incorrect top-1 matches alike score above a cosine similarity of 0.7 on every backbone.

Nor is the encoder the bottleneck. Fine-tuning a batch-normalisation neck adds about a point of closed-set mAP, and the best configuration at most 3.4. MeCha adds at most a point. The correct crops are present in the stream; what fails is the rule that admits them to the gallery.

The commit condition

A change to the gallery can alter the answer only for a query whose nearest entry the change supplied. We call these queries captured. The effect of any change on identity Rank-1 then decomposes exactly as

Δ = c (peff − a+)

where c is the share of queries the change captures, peff the share of captured queries whose capturing entry carries their own identity, and a+ the accuracy the unchanged gallery would have achieved on those same queries. A change improves recognition exactly when peff > a+, which we call the commit condition.

Neither term is the one intuition reaches for. The natural test compares how often the added crops are correct with the gallery's average accuracy. Additions that pass a page-level check, introduced below, are correct 86.9 percent of the time on POPCharacters, yet their effective precision peff is only 31 to 48 percent, because a crop assigned to the right character still captures other characters' queries. The captured queries also tend to lie where the gallery already performs well, so a+ exceeds the average accuracy wherever the gallery is strong. MagiV2 on Manga109 shows the consequence. Its gallery starts at 64.8 identity Rank-1, its page-checked additions reach a peff of 54.4 against an a+ of 67.8, and growth lowers identity Rank-1 by 6.23 points, exactly as the decomposition predicts. The intuitive test would have predicted a gain.

Measured on a labelled half of a corpus, the three terms make the correct decision for the other half in every random split wherever the change matters. The condition also governs changes that add no crop at all. Restricting each query's candidates to the characters of the last twenty crops gains 3.4 to 8.0 points on four of five backbones, and costs MagiV2 7.2 points on Manga109, where its gallery is strongest.

Vouch: grow only where the page vouches

The commit condition turns gallery growth into a design problem: raise peff, and act only where the additions will be correct more often than the gallery they join. Vouch makes three changes to the gallery and fits no parameters to data.

A cast sheet
Each character is one ℓ2-normalised running average of the crops assigned to it, so an addition refines its character's entry instead of creating a rival entry that captures other characters' queries. This acts on c.
Commitment under the page constraint
A crop joins a character only when another crop in its page group is already assigned to that character. The page groups come from MagiV2's character-affinity head, run once per page without training, and two crops it groups depict the same character 89.9 percent of the time. The rule raises peff, at the price of abstaining wherever the page offers no evidence.
Seed expansion
Before the stream starts, the same page groups add the crops on a seed's page that share its character, 1.43 per seed on average, 90.6 percent of them correct.

With five random seeds per character, the cast sheet and commitment together add 3.3 to 6.5 points of identity Rank-1 over the static gallery, significant on all five backbones and 28 to 41 percent of the oracle's gain. With one seed, seed expansion adds 4.7 to 6.8 points on all but MagiV2. Zero-shot on Manga109, where MagiV2's gallery starts strongest, MagiV2 retains only the cast sheet, as the condition predicts.

Where the supervision lies

When the seeds are each character's first appearances, these changes have little to act on, and the reason is the position of the labels. In the opening quarter of the stream, a fifth of the last twenty crops read are labelled; afterwards, fewer than one in twenty. A typical query lies four times further from its nearest seed than under random seeding, and that distance accounts for almost all of the gap between the two regimes.

Binding supplies the missing reference. A frozen embedding groups a page's crops above one similarity threshold, page groups then merge in reading order into the best earlier match above a second threshold or form a new group, and a group's first seed labels it. No detector or panel geometry is involved. With MagiV2's embedding as a shared binder, first-appearance seeding gains 12.5 to 16.9 points of identity Rank-1 on every backbone on POPCharacters, about two thirds of the oracle's gain, and 8.0 to 10.4 points on four of five backbones on Manga109. The propagated identity is what helps, since adding the same crops under shuffled identities lowers accuracy. When each backbone binds with its own embedding, the predicted Δ rather than the measured gain chooses the threshold, and the condition anticipates the one case that loses: MagiV2 on Manga109, whose gallery starts above the binder's precision, at 57.5 against 47.8.

What it shows

Re:Cognize separates two problems that conventional Re-ID evaluation conflates: recognising an identity, and maintaining trustworthy evidence of it. The first is within reach of current backbones. The second is not, and because a single wrong addition shapes every later decision, the quality of a model's memory matters as much as the quality of its representation. Abstention is the mechanism, since every gain we measure comes from a rule that chooses when to act. Without fitting any parameter, Vouch recovers between one third and two thirds of the improvement that perfect labels would provide.

Our claims concern identity maintenance. All experiments use Japanese comics and ground-truth crops, and the corpora do not yet test appearance changes across long story arcs, which leaves Western comics and webtoons for future work. Two directions follow. Dialogue offers names that could tie appearances to persistent identities, and the commit condition applies wherever a system grows its own reference set, in tracking and in self-training alike.
