    Essay

    Comics are a modality of their own

    A comic tells a story in a few still images and leaves everything in between to the reader. Readers fill that gap without effort. Models do not, and that makes comics a revealing test of what a vision-language model can follow.

    The maintainers

    Sparse

    A page shows a few moments of a story, not a continuous stream, so a model must infer what happens between panels.

    Lettered

    The text lives inside the art, in balloons, captions and sound effects, and it belongs to a speaker the page may never name.

    Long

    A character, a promise or a cause can be set up hundreds of pages before it matters, and the reader is expected to remember it.

    Between two panels, time passes, a character moves across a room, a decision is made; none of it is drawn. Research on comics is older than deep learning, and it has never had one home. Panel and balloon detection grew up in document analysis. Colorization and screentones belong to graphics. Speaker attribution borrows from NLP. Accessible readers come from HCI, and the study of comics as a medium from the digital humanities. Each community built its own datasets and gave its own names to the same problems, so a newcomer has to rediscover the field one venue at a time.

    Vision-language models have changed the questions. Models now answer questions about pages, translate with the art in view, transcribe whole chapters with speaker names, and draw comics from a script. Benchmarks ask whether a model is reading the page or recalling a story it already knows.

    What this list is

    It is a curated map of that work, built to be broad. "Comics" here means manga, manhwa, manhua, webtoons, bandes dessinées, comic strips, graphic novels and single-panel cartoons. A paper belongs when comics are its subject or its main test bed, whether it reads them, searches them, translates them, describes them for blind readers, draws them, restores them, animates them or uses them to probe a model.

    Earlier lists did valuable work, and we credit and link them below. Some have paused, and others centre on anime and illustration. We want a list that stays current, so we built it to be maintained. Every entry is a record in one data file, checked against arXiv, Crossref or the venue's own pages, and the README and this page are generated from that file.

    How to help

    If your paper is missing, a link is broken, or a topic should exist, open an issue or a pull request. The contributing guide takes two minutes to read. We would rather be told than be wrong.

    How a paper gets in

    The rule is code, and a monthly refresh applies it.

    1. A conference or journal ranked A* or A by CORECORE2023 for conferences, CORE2020 for journals
    2. A workshop, or a peer-reviewed track of such a conferenceFindings, technical briefs, extended abstracts
    3. A dataset or benchmark paper, at any published venueThe resources the field builds on
    4. An arXiv preprint, for four monthsIt stays if a qualifying venue publishes it
    5. A survey, peer-reviewed or cited at least 20 timesSurveys take longer to publish
